## At a glance

- **My role:** frontend developer on a team of about ten. My commits are in the React app (30 commits, July 2026). On the backend, I worked with the API team as a consumer of its endpoints, building and testing the screens on top of them.
- **Frontend:** React 19, TypeScript, Vite, Redux Toolkit, React Router 7, Tailwind CSS, Socket.IO client, Vitest, Docker and Nginx
- **Backend:** Node.js, Express, TypeScript, PostgreSQL with Prisma, Redis and BullMQ, Socket.IO, Azure Blob Storage, Jest
- **Scale of the codebase:** about 790 frontend commits and 520 backend commits across the team, 89 database models and 128 backend test files

## The problem

Without one shared system, a firm's matters, deadlines and documents end up spread across tools, and clients have to call the firm to ask what's happening with their case or which document is still needed.

The platform brings this into one place for Indian law firms, with three portals:

- **Platform admins** onboard firms and define workflow templates for each type of matter.
- **Firm users** (admins and employees) manage matters, clients, tasks, hearings, documents, reports and billing. A person can belong to several firms and picks one after login.
- **Clients** log in with their phone number, follow their matters stage by stage, upload requested documents and see upcoming hearings.

## What I built on the frontend

### A customizable dashboard

The firm dashboard is a grid of widgets users can rearrange by dragging. I worked on the dashboard shell and its widget management.

- On mobile, the grid library has its own touch handling that ignores the normal "draggable" setting, so dragging kept triggering while users tried to scroll. **I traced it to the library's internal touch sensor** and fixed it by marking every widget as static on mobile, so the dashboard scrolls normally and stays arranged.
- I tried long-press to drag on mobile first, then removed it when it clashed with scrolling. The simpler behaviour worked better.

### Firm-admin screens that work on phones

Firm users need the app on their phones too, not only at a desk. I made the firm-admin modules responsive: the matter list and matter view, clients, documents and document requests, tasks, workflow templates, calendar, employees and reports.

- Filters move into a mobile filter drawer instead of a row of dropdowns.
- Headers and action buttons rearrange for small screens.
- Tables keep working with the pagination bar and rows-per-page controls.

## The platform we built

![Architecture diagram](/images/projects/legal-platform/architecture.jpg)

As a team, the backend was designed around a few key ideas:

- **Multi-tenant by design.** Every table carries the firm it belongs to, so one firm can never see another firm's data, and users who work for several firms switch between them safely.
- **Strong sign-in.** JWT access and refresh tokens, a second step with OTP or an authenticator app, and Google and Microsoft single sign-on.
- **Permissions on every route.** Each endpoint declares the permission it needs, and roles are configured per firm.
- **Workflows that drive matters.** A matter follows a template of ordered stages. Tasks, events and documents can be linked to a stage, and each document can be marked visible to the client or firm-only.
- **Work that runs in the background.** Bulk matter imports run on a Redis queue with a separate worker. Scheduled jobs send hearing reminders, flag overdue tasks and refresh court data.
- **Real-time messaging** between clients and firms with Socket.IO, and documents stored in Azure Blob Storage.

## What I learned

- **Working in a large team** means reading more code than writing it, and keeping changes small and easy to review.
- **Mobile isn't a smaller desktop.** The drag-and-drop bug only appeared on real touch devices, and the right fix was to change the behaviour, not to fight the library.
- **Building against a real API** made me pay attention to loading, empty and error states, and to how permissions shape what each user can see.
